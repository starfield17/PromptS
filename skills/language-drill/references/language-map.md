# Language Map

Read this when the learner asks for modules, a sequence of practice, or help choosing the next structure. The map is a selection aid inherited from Hazel, not a prerequisite or a compulsory curriculum. Adapt the categories to the target language; English constructions are not universal.

| Range | Modules |
|---|---|
| 1–6 · Foundation | 1 Identity; 2 Location; 3 Possession; 4 Quantity and description; 5 Wants and needs; 6 Daily actions |
| 7–13 · Expansion | 7 Time and routine; 8 Past actions; 9 Future and plans; 10 Ability and permission; 11 Requests and offers; 12 Preferences and comparison; 13 Feelings and states |
| 14–21 · Structure | 14 Reasons and cause; 15 Conditions; 16 Obligation and advice; 17 Experience; 18 Passive; 19 Reported speech; 20 Relative clauses; 21 Purpose and result |
| 22–30 · Fluency | 22 Hypotheticals; 23 Concession and contrast; 24 Opinion and stance; 25 Agreement and disagreement; 26 Narration; 27 Summarizing; 28 Persuasion; 29 Debate and rebuttal; 30 Abstract expression |

Choose a structure the learner currently needs and can almost produce. Module numbers do not determine proficiency; a learner can be fluent in narration and still hesitate over requests.

For English module 15, a useful first exercise might be: “If it rains, we'll stay home.” Cue: “the train is late / take a taxi.” Wait for the learner's sentence before adding another cue. If they confuse this with hypothetical conditionals, contrast the intended meaning before increasing variation.
