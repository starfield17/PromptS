# Example: Offshore SaaS

> Illustrative example only. Current market facts, valuations, guidance, and company data must be verified before use.

## Direction

Offshore SaaS refers to software companies selling cloud-based products outside their home market, often using remote-first distribution and lower-cost engineering.

## Trigger

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Trigger | A company reports accelerating overseas revenue or improving net retention in foreign markets. |
| Trigger Type | Customer Adoption / Financial |
| Why Now | Global distribution is easier, but competition and localization costs remain high. |
| First-order Impact | SaaS vendors with exportable workflows. |
| Second-order Impact | payments, compliance, localization, cloud infrastructure, channel partners. |
| Real Variable Changed? | Source needed: overseas ARR, NRR, CAC payback, churn. |

## Problem Solved

The direction exists because many business workflows are similar across geographies, but buyers want cheaper, faster, or more specialized software than incumbent local vendors provide.

## Value Chain Map

| Layer | Problem Solved by Layer | Representative Players | Bottleneck Level | Profit Pool Quality | Competition Intensity | Commoditization Risk | Investment Judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core SaaS product | Automates workflow | product vendors | Medium | High if sticky | High | Medium | Good only with retention and pricing power. |
| Distribution / channel | Acquires customers in new markets | direct sales, partners, marketplaces | High | Medium | High | Medium | Often the real bottleneck. |
| Localization / compliance | Makes product usable by region | internal teams, partners | Medium | Medium | Medium | Low–Medium | Can protect position in complex markets. |
| Payments / billing | Supports monetization | payment providers | Low–Medium | Medium | High | Medium | Infrastructure layer, not always investable. |
| Customer success | Retention and expansion | internal / outsourced teams | Medium | Medium | Medium | Low | Important for NRR and churn. |

## Profit Pool

The highest-quality profit pool belongs to SaaS products with high retention, clear ROI, low incremental delivery cost, and defensible distribution. The weakest layer is generic distribution without product differentiation.

## Market Consensus

Assumed consensus, pending source validation:

```text
The market often values offshore SaaS on international TAM expansion and lower engineering cost.
```

## Variant View

```text
The market may overestimate TAM portability and underestimate distribution friction. The key variable is not whether the product can be sold globally, but whether customer acquisition and retention remain attractive outside the home market.
```

## Initial Judgment

```text
C. Deep Dive
```

The direction can be attractive if international growth preserves SaaS unit economics. The next step is to separate genuine repeatable distribution from temporary growth driven by underpriced sales effort.

## Falsification Conditions

1. Overseas growth requires disproportionate sales and support spending.
2. NRR falls outside the home market.
3. Churn rises as customers mature.
4. Local competitors copy features and undercut price.
5. Compliance and localization costs reduce operating leverage.
