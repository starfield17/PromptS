# Example: Humanoid Robotics

> Illustrative example only. Current market facts, valuations, guidance, and company data must be verified before use.

## Direction

Humanoid robotics attempts to automate flexible physical tasks in environments where fixed industrial automation is too rigid or expensive.

## Trigger

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Trigger | New product demos, pilot deployments, or large corporate commitments. |
| Trigger Type | Technology / Customer Adoption / Narrative |
| Why Now | AI perception and control models may improve robot generalization, while labor scarcity creates buyer interest. |
| First-order Impact | robot OEMs, actuators, reducers, sensors, batteries, control systems. |
| Second-order Impact | manufacturing automation, logistics, safety certification, robot maintenance. |
| Real Variable Changed? | Source needed: pilot orders, unit economics, deployment hours, failure rates. |

## Problem Solved

The direction exists because many physical tasks are repetitive but not standardized enough for traditional automation. Buyers pay if humanoids reduce labor cost, improve uptime, or add flexibility without rebuilding the work environment.

## Value Chain Map

| Layer | Problem Solved by Layer | Representative Players | Bottleneck Level | Profit Pool Quality | Competition Intensity | Commoditization Risk | Investment Judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Robot OEM / system | Integrates robot into usable product | Robot developers, automation firms | Medium | Mixed | High | Medium | High narrative, economics unproven. |
| Actuators / motors | Enables motion and force control | Component suppliers | High | Medium–High | Medium | Medium | Key bottleneck if reliability and cost targets are hard. |
| Reducers / transmission | Converts motor output into controlled motion | Precision component suppliers | High | Medium–High | Medium | Medium | Potential profit pool if precision and durability are scarce. |
| Sensors | Perception and feedback | Vision, force, IMU suppliers | Medium | Medium | High | High | Many components may commoditize. |
| Battery / power | Runtime and weight constraints | Battery suppliers | Medium | Medium | High | Medium–High | Important but may be pass-through unless specialized. |
| AI control software | Generalization and task learning | model/control stack providers | High | Potentially High | High | High | Attractive if proprietary data loop exists. |
| Deployment / service | Integration, maintenance, uptime | integrators, service providers | Medium | Medium | Medium | Low–Medium | Underappreciated if robots require heavy field support. |

## Profit Pool

The best profit pool may not be the robot OEM. It may sit in scarce precision components, control software with data feedback loops, or deployment services if real-world reliability remains difficult.

## Initial Judgment

```text
B. Watch
```

The problem is real, but investability depends on evidence that pilots convert into repeat deployments with acceptable reliability and unit economics.

## Falsification Conditions

1. Demos do not convert into paid deployments.
2. Unit cost remains too high for target use cases.
3. Reliability metrics fail to improve.
4. Component suppliers face price pressure before volumes scale.
5. Customers choose cheaper fixed automation or software-only alternatives.
