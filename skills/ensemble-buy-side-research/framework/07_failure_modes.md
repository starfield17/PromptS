# Failure Modes

## 1. Theme Addiction

Symptom:

```text
The memo talks about how large the opportunity is but not who captures profit.
```

Fix:

```text
Force value-chain and profit-pool sections before company conclusions.
```

## 2. Stock List Instead of Value Chain

Symptom:

```text
The output lists companies without explaining their layer or economics.
```

Fix:

```text
Every company must be mapped to a layer and role.
```

## 3. Consensus Omission

Symptom:

```text
The memo states a thesis without explaining what the market already believes.
```

Fix:

```text
Add a Market Consensus section before Variant View.
```

## 4. Fake Contrarianism

Symptom:

```text
The memo says the market is wrong but does not define a measurable disagreement.
```

Fix:

```text
Require a variable, time horizon, and falsification condition.
```

## 5. Revenue Equals Profit Fallacy

Symptom:

```text
The output assumes high revenue growth means good investment economics.
```

Fix:

```text
Evaluate margin, bargaining power, capex, and competition.
```

## 6. Ignoring Losers

Symptom:

```text
Only beneficiaries are listed.
```

Fix:

```text
Add potential losers and substitution effects.
```

## 7. No Time Horizon

Symptom:

```text
The thesis has no timing.
```

Fix:

```text
Separate 1–3 months, 3–12 months, and 1–3 years.
```

## 8. No Data Watchlist

Symptom:

```text
The memo ends with a view but no way to track it.
```

Fix:

```text
Create a variable table with sources and thresholds.
```

## 9. Unsupported Current Facts

Symptom:

```text
The memo claims current market consensus, price, or management guidance without verification.
```

Fix:

```text
Label as assumption or source-needed.
```

## 10. Over-precise Scoring

Symptom:

```text
Scores imply false accuracy.
```

Fix:

```text
Use scores as forcing functions, not mathematical truth.
```

## 11. Generic Sell-side Tone

Symptom:

```text
The memo uses phrases such as "broad prospects" or "expected to develop rapidly."
```

Fix:

```text
Rewrite into problem, economics, variables, and falsification.
```

## 12. No Decision

Symptom:

```text
The memo provides information but no judgment.
```

Fix:

```text
End with A/B/C/D judgment and next research steps.
```


## 13. Moat Label Without Evidence

Symptom:

```text
The memo says a company has a moat but does not show pricing power, switching cost, cost advantage, retention, brand/share-of-mind, or other observable evidence.
```

Fix:

```text
Add a Buffett Quality Lens section with moat source, moat trend, and evidence still needed.
```

## 14. Cheapness Substitutes for Quality

Symptom:

```text
The thesis depends on a low valuation but the underlying business has weak returns, no reinvestment runway, or deteriorating economics.
```

Fix:

```text
Classify it as cigar butt / turnaround / cyclical trade, define catalyst and exit, and do not call it a durable thesis unless quality evidence improves.
```

## 15. Outside-the-Circle Confidence

Symptom:

```text
The memo gives a confident conclusion even though the decisive variables are not understandable or source-backed.
```

Fix:

```text
Mark circle of competence as Edge or Outside, downgrade judgment, and list the specific unknowns.
```

## 16. Small Upside, Fatal Downside

Symptom:

```text
The thesis offers modest upside but contains leverage, liquidity, governance, refinancing, or tail risks that can permanently impair capital.
```

Fix:

```text
Apply a ruin-risk override and downgrade regardless of score.
```
