# Buffett Quality Lens

This lens translates Buffett's 1998 University of Florida investing principles into the ensemble buy-side research workflow. Use it after profit-pool analysis and before the final judgment.

## Purpose

The existing framework asks whether a direction is economically real, where profit pools sit, and where consensus may be wrong. The Buffett lens adds a stricter owner-oriented filter:

```text
Do I understand the business?
Does the layer or company have durable economics?
Is the moat stable or widening?
Can management allocate capital sensibly?
Is the price reasonable relative to long-term owner earnings?
Can I survive being wrong without permanent capital impairment?
Would I still want to own it if the market closed for five years?
```

## 1. Circle of Competence Gate

Classify the idea before scoring it:

```text
Inside: economics, customers, competitors, and risks are understandable.
Edge: some drivers are knowable, but one or more critical variables remain unclear.
Outside: the thesis depends on forecasting technology, macro, regulation, or competitive behavior the analyst cannot reasonably understand.
```

Rules:

```text
Inside ideas can proceed to thesis work.
Edge ideas can become Watch or Deep Dive only if the unknowns are explicitly tracked.
Outside ideas should not become Potential Investment Thesis, even if the narrative is attractive.
```

## 2. Business Ownership Test

Analyze the security as ownership in a business, not as a ticker or catalyst.

Ask:

```text
What would I earn if I owned the whole business?
How much incremental capital is required to grow?
Are profits cash-like or accounting-like?
Who has bargaining power: the company, customers, suppliers, or regulators?
```

Prefer:

```text
recurring demand
high return on incremental capital
low capital intensity relative to growth
pricing power
customer trust or habit
long runway without constant reinvention
```

Avoid:

```text
low-return businesses bought only because they look cheap
capital-intensive growth with poor incremental returns
revenue growth that leaks to customers, suppliers, or competitors
```

## 3. Moat and Widening Test

A moat is not a slogan. It must appear in evidence.

Possible moat sources:

```text
brand / share of mind
low-cost position
network or ecosystem effects
switching cost
distribution advantage
scale procurement or scale data advantage
process know-how
regulatory license
scarce asset or location
```

Widening evidence:

```text
stable or rising gross margin despite competition
improving retention or repeat purchase
rising share without price discounting
lower unit cost versus peers
customer willingness to pay a premium
competitors failing despite capital access
```

Narrowing evidence:

```text
price promotion becomes necessary
substitutes gain credibility
customers multi-source or self-build
gross margins fall while revenue rises
supplier count grows faster than demand
brand loses share of mind
```

## 4. Quality Before Cheapness

Cheapness alone is not enough. Cigar-butt opportunities may work once, but they should not be confused with durable compounding.

Use this distinction:

```text
Cigar butt: one-time value realization, weak business, limited reinvestment runway.
Compounder: durable business, strong incremental returns, long reinvestment runway.
Turnaround: requires operational change; treat as speculative until evidence appears.
```

A cheap weak business can be Watch or tactical Deep Dive, but should rarely be called Potential Investment Thesis unless the catalyst, downside, and exit are explicit.

## 5. Five-Year Market Closure Test

Before labeling an idea investable, ask:

```text
If the exchange closed for five years, would I be comfortable owning this business based on its economics, balance sheet, and competitive position?
```

If the answer depends mainly on near-term price action, sentiment, or multiple expansion, downgrade the judgment.

## 6. Downside and Ruin-Risk Test

Never risk what matters for what does not matter. Treat leverage, refinancing, customer concentration, regulatory cliffs, and fraud/governance risk as thesis-level issues.

Red flags:

```text
thesis requires high leverage to work
liquidity depends on friendly capital markets
small upside but existential downside
management incentives reward growth over returns
opaque balance sheet or off-balance-sheet risk
model output hides non-normal tail risk
```

## 7. Macro Humility

Macro can be important but unknowable. Do not make the thesis depend on precise forecasts for GDP, rates, currencies, or policy timing unless those variables are explicitly part of the watchlist and downside case.

Prefer knowable variables:

```text
unit economics
customer adoption
pricing
margin
capacity
inventory
cash conversion
retention
competitive share
```

## 8. Buffett Lens Output Block

Every full memo should include:

```text
Circle of competence:
Business quality:
Moat source:
Moat trend: widening / stable / narrowing / unknown
Pricing power:
Capital intensity:
Management / capital allocation:
Five-year market closure test:
Downside / ruin risk:
Buffett-lens implication: upgrade / neutral / downgrade / pass
Evidence still needed:
```

## 9. Decision Impact

Use this override logic:

```text
Outside circle of competence → cannot be Potential Investment Thesis.
No durable moat and high commoditization risk → normally Watch or Discard.
High leverage or ruin risk → downgrade regardless of upside narrative.
Wonderful business but no valuation sanity → Watch, not immediate thesis.
Wonderful business plus measurable consensus gap plus valuation sanity → Deep Dive or Potential Investment Thesis.
```
