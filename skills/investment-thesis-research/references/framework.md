# Skills Collection Framework

This file defines the modular “Skills” behind the System Prompt. They are conceptual modules, not rigid steps. Invoke only the modules that fit the question.

## 1. Signal Intake Skill

**Purpose:** Turn a vague topic into a concrete research question.

**Use when:** The user provides a theme, headline, stock move, policy, product announcement, earnings quote, or market rumor.

**Input:** Direction, trigger, user notes, known facts.

**Process:**
- Identify what happened.
- Identify why it matters now.
- Classify the trigger: demand, supply, cost, policy, technology, liquidity, valuation, or narrative.
- Convert the trigger into one or two investable questions.

**Output:**
- Direction.
- Trigger.
- Why now.
- Core research question.

**Avoid:** Beginning with a conclusion before understanding the trigger.

---

## 2. Problem Translation Skill

**Purpose:** Translate a market theme into the real economic problem being solved.

**Use when:** A theme sounds abstract, buzzword-heavy, or concept-driven.

**Process:**
- Ask what pain point, bottleneck, scarcity, or inefficiency is being addressed.
- Identify who pays for the solution.
- Identify whether the value comes from cost reduction, productivity, revenue, substitution, compliance, risk reduction, or capacity expansion.
- Ask why the problem becomes urgent now.

**Output:**
- The real-world problem.
- Economic buyer.
- Value creation mechanism.
- Urgency driver.

**Avoid:** Treating a technology or concept as inherently investable.

---

## 3. Value-Chain Decomposition Skill

**Purpose:** Map the sector into layers so the analysis can find where value is actually captured.

**Use when:** The topic involves an industry chain, ecosystem, supply bottleneck, platform, or multi-layer technology stack.

**Process:**
- Build 5–9 layers depending on the sector.
- For each layer, define the problem solved.
- Identify upstream dependencies and downstream monetization.
- Mark bottleneck layers and commodity layers.
- If relevant, split global leaders and regional/local substitutes.

**Output table columns:**
- Layer.
- Problem solved.
- Global/core players.
- Local/regional players.
- Economics / bargaining power.
- Competitive intensity.
- Investment read.

**Avoid:** Listing companies without explaining their position in the chain.

---

## 4. Player and Exposure Mapping Skill

**Purpose:** Separate real beneficiaries from weak concept exposure.

**Use when:** The market is chasing a theme and many names are being grouped together.

**Process:**
- Classify players into direct beneficiaries, second-order beneficiaries, enablers, customers, potential losers, and concept-only exposure.
- Determine whether the company earns revenue from the theme today or only has narrative association.
- Identify customer concentration and dependency risk.

**Output:**
- Beneficiaries.
- Hurt or pressured parties.
- Ambiguous / optionality names.
- Concept-only names.

**Avoid:** Assuming every company in the theme benefits equally.

---

## 5. Profit Pool and Bargaining Power Skill

**Purpose:** Find where the attractive economics are.

**Use when:** The topic requires investment judgment, not just description.

**Process:**
- Identify layers with high margins, scarce capacity, technical barriers, customer lock-in, ecosystem control, or regulatory protection.
- Identify layers with fast commoditization, pricing pressure, low switching costs, or high capex intensity.
- Ask whether high margins are structural, cyclical, or temporary.
- Identify who owns the bottleneck and who funds the bottleneck.

**Output:**
- Best profit pool.
- Most crowded layer.
- Most fragile economics.
- Margin sustainability assessment.

**Avoid:** Confusing revenue growth with profit capture.

---

## 6. Consensus Narrative Parsing Skill

**Purpose:** Identify what the market probably already believes.

**Use when:** The user asks whether a direction is worth researching or whether the market is missing something.

**Process:**
- Summarize the mainstream story in one paragraph.
- Identify what assumptions must hold for that story to be true.
- Identify whether the narrative is driven by fundamentals, positioning, liquidity, scarcity, policy, or hype.
- Note which parts may already be priced in.

**Output:**
- Market consensus narrative.
- Embedded assumptions.
- What may already be priced.

**Avoid:** Calling a view “contrarian” without first defining consensus.

---

## 7. Non-Consensus Insight Skill

**Purpose:** Generate a plausible differentiated angle.

**Use when:** The user wants “what might I see earlier than the market?”

**Process:**
- Look for second-order beneficiaries.
- Look for bottlenecks the market underestimates.
- Look for margin, ROIC, cash-flow, or capacity constraints hidden behind revenue growth.
- Look for timing mismatch between narrative and fundamentals.
- Look for regional substitution or policy protection that can create profit pools even without global leadership.
- Look for cost curve changes that may unlock demand.

**Output:**
- Potential non-consensus angle.
- Why it could matter.
- Evidence needed to support it.

**Avoid:** Forcing contrarianism. Sometimes consensus is mostly right.

---

## 8. Key Variable Watchlist Skill

**Purpose:** Turn a thesis into measurable variables.

**Use when:** The analysis needs to become trackable over time.

**Process:**
- Pick 3–10 variables with the highest causal power.
- Separate leading indicators from lagging indicators.
- Include both confirmation and disconfirmation variables.
- Prefer variables that can be checked repeatedly.

**Output:**
- Variable.
- Why it matters.
- Direction that confirms thesis.
- Direction that weakens thesis.
- Possible data source.

**Avoid:** Making a long data wishlist with no prioritization.

---

## 9. Evidence Plan Skill

**Purpose:** Define exactly what to verify next.

**Use when:** The current analysis is preliminary or evidence is incomplete.

**Process:**
- List the next data to check: filings, earnings calls, orders, prices, capex, utilization, inventories, supply data, policy, customer behavior, channel checks.
- Assign each evidence item to the hypothesis it tests.
- Separate public data from hard-to-get channel checks.

**Output:**
- Evidence checklist.
- Source type.
- Hypothesis tested.
- Priority.

**Avoid:** Letting the answer end as opinion without a verification path.

---

## 10. Triage and Judgment Skill

**Purpose:** Convert research into an actionable research status.

**Use when:** The user asks what to do with a theme or whether to keep researching.

**Process:**
- Assess mechanism clarity, profit-pool quality, evidence strength, valuation risk, and disconfirmation risk.
- Choose one status:
  - Discard,
  - Watch,
  - Deep dive,
  - Potential investment hypothesis.
- Explain the reason in one paragraph.

**Output:**
- Status.
- Reason.
- Next action.

**Avoid:** Giving a confident investment conclusion when the evidence only supports watching.

---

## 11. Disconfirmation Skill

**Purpose:** Protect the analyst from thesis lock-in.

**Use when:** Any provisional judgment is made.

**Process:**
- Ask what data would prove the thesis wrong.
- Identify timing risks.
- Identify pricing-in risks.
- Identify hidden cost, competition, or policy risks.
- State what would force a downgrade or abandonment.

**Output:**
- Kill conditions.
- Warning signals.
- Data that would change the view.

**Avoid:** Only listing upside catalysts.

---

## 12. Synthesis Skill

**Purpose:** Produce the final memo.

**Use when:** The analysis needs to be presented clearly.

**Process:**
- Compress the analysis into a memo with clear sections.
- Keep the table useful but not bloated.
- Preserve facts, consensus, differential view, variables, judgment, and kill conditions.
- Use plain English and analyst-style bullets.

**Output:**
- Research memo.
- Value-chain table.
- Watchlist.
- Preliminary judgment.

**Avoid:** Writing a long report that buries the conclusion.
