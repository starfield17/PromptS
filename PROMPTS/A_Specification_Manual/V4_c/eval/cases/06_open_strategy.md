# Case 06: Open strategy question (ceiling test)

**Tests**: Given an open question, how high does each prompt let the model reach? This is the direct test of V2's "conceptual density sets the ceiling" against V4's "precision of the target sets the ceiling" (V4 P3). Score **insight** heavily here.
**A good answer**: Leads with a point of view, not a menu. Among the non-obvious points it might make:
- Brightline is keeping the rural and long-haul lanes, which are probably the least profitable, and taking the dense metro routes. The margin hit is therefore likely worse than the 40% volume figure suggests.
- The 34 dedicated tractors and their drivers need a plan: redeploy, sell, or shrink by attrition, keeping driver retention in mind.
- Fixed costs will be spread over less volume.
- There is a pricing lever: reprice the remaining lanes before the renewal in 14 months, while Northwind still has leverage.
- Diversification: reduce the dependence on one customer, which is 28% of revenue today.
- Possible counter-offers to Brightline, such as surge or backup capacity for its new private fleet.

It should also name one or two ways the plan could fail, and the first two or three actions to take this month.
**Red flags**: Generic advice ("diversify your customer base," "improve service quality") with no connection to the specifics. A long, unprioritized list. Manifesto tone.

## Paste as the user message

```text
<materials>
Customer: Brightline Home Supply. 28% of Northwind revenue; 34 dedicated tractors; contract renews in 14 months.

Brightline's notice (summary): Over the next 18 months, Brightline will move about 40% of its current Northwind volume to a new private fleet serving its densest metro routes (Columbus, Cincinnati). Rural and long-haul lanes stay with Northwind "for now."
</materials>

What should we do?
```
